# What actors search for, and what I can actually show them

Data from actorrise.com search logs, 20 March to 13 September 2026.
2,173 searches, 537 actors.

---

## The short version

173 searches asked for a contemporary monologue. 83% of what I was able to put
in front of those actors was written before 1970. None of it was a contemporary
play, because I do not have a single one.

---

## The demand

| | |
|---|---|
| Searches asking for contemporary work | **173 of 2,173 (8.0%)** |
| Actors who asked at least once | **71 of 537 (13%)** |

That is one in eight actors on the site, asking in their own words, unprompted.

They are specific about it. Real searches, typed exactly like this:

- "middle-aged man, contemporary, naturalistic, lonely, stuck, regret, family, marriage"
- "contemporary monologue, potentially about business, ideally a dark comedy, for a cunning..."
- "hyper emotional monologue for 21 year old male, contemporary and at least 2min"
- "Find a comedic contemporary monologue, no longer than 90 seconds, for a character between..."
- "Female lead / leading-woman energy. Ideally 18 to 25. Contemporary American/natural accent"

## What they got

Every result those 173 searches returned, by what it actually is:

| What the actor saw | Results | Share |
|---|---|---|
| A play written before 1970 | 709 | 45.8% |
| A play with no date, which means public domain, so pre-1929 | 578 | 37.3% |
| Film or television, contemporary but the wrong medium | 261 | 16.9% |
| **A contemporary play** | **0** | **0%** |

83% of what I showed them was at least 56 years old. The 17% that was genuinely
contemporary was screen work, which most theatre auditions will not accept.

## Why I did not catch this sooner

By every measure I had, these searches were succeeding. Contemporary searches
score *better* than the average search on the site:

| | Contemporary searches | Every other search |
|---|---|---|
| Flagged as a weak match | 5% | 21% |
| Returned nothing at all | 2 | — |
| Average match confidence | 0.462 | 0.421 |

The actor types "contemporary monologue", gets twenty confident results, and
the system records a win. It only shows up when somebody writes in. Four did
in the last four days:

> "these arent contemporary"
> "A warm-up contemporary"
> "A more contemporary piece, with a bit of comedy and edge, teen to early twenties character"

## Titles actors have asked me for by name

These came in through the request form, so an actor went looking for a specific
play and did not find it:

- **Rosencrantz and Guildenstern Are Dead**, Tom Stoppard, which is licensed
  through you on behalf of Samuel French
- **Speech and Debate**, Stephen Karam, a Dramatists Play Service title
- **Heathers**
- **Mean Girls**

Two of those four are yours. An actor went looking for them on my site and
found nothing.

## What I have

19,400 monologues, all public domain, mostly Gutenberg and Perseus. Every one
is a full text I extracted myself and can show in full. I hold no scripts I do
not have the right to hold, and nothing on the site was written after 1929
except screen work.

The gap is not something I can scrape my way out of. Every contemporary play is
in copyright, which is the entire point of the conversation.
